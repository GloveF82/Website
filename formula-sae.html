As a member of USC's competitive Formula SAE Electric program, I contribute to two concurrent sub-teams — Ergonomics and Vehicle Dynamics — working to design, manufacture, and validate systems on the team's electric open-wheel race car. The program competes under SAE International rules, requiring strict compliance in safety, packaging, and performance across every subsystem.

Ergonomics Team

Pedal Box Design & Braking System

On the Ergonomics Team, I was responsible for the design and analysis of the driver interface — specifically the pedal box assembly including the brake pedal, brake bias bar, master cylinders, and mounting plate. The USC-branded aluminum pedal plate was modeled in Siemens NX, balancing lightweighting (patterned cutouts and slots) with stiffness requirements under driver braking loads.

Beyond CAD, I participated in the hands-on assembly and integration of braking system components, including installing rotors and calipers, routing and bleeding brake lines, packaging ABS hardware within the cockpit, and fabricating a heel stop for the pedal box. The pedal box is adjustable for driver fit and mounts within the tight ergonomic envelope defined by FSAE rules.

Ergonomics Team

Custom Driver Seat

A critical ergonomics deliverable was the design and fabrication of a custom-molded driver seat fitted directly to the driver's body. The seat was formed using an expanding foam-in-bag process inside the cockpit, creating a conforming shape that distributes driver loads and satisfies FSAE lateral support requirements. The first mold attempt failed — the foam did not expand evenly and the resulting shape did not meet the required geometry or support profile. The process was redone from scratch, refining the bag placement and foam quantity to achieve a proper fit. The images show the successful second mold, which was then used as a plug to layup the final carbon fiber seat shell.

Vehicle Dynamics Team

Steering System

The steering system was designed for both performance and compliance, featuring a NARRco steering rack (365.76mm, 87.9mm/rev), rubber U-joints with 30° capability, radial ball bearings, and 15.875mm OD steel 4130 column tubes. The steering wheel uses a 3D printed Nylon 12CF main body with an aluminum 6061-T4 back panel, COTS paddle shifters, and two buttons for comms and launch control. The design achieved 110% pro-Ackerman, a 2.4m turning radius, and 78.74mm pitman trail — validated through driver torque measurements showing no performance loss versus prior geometry. During installation, shims were used on the steering rack mounting to precisely set the rack position and prevent the rack housing from contacting the chassis frame under load.

Vehicle Dynamics Team

Suspension & Uprights

On the Vehicle Dynamics Team, I performed suspension system design and analysis for the electric car, calculating shock absorber requirements from vehicle mass and load transfer data derived from cornering, braking, and bump scenarios. I assisted with the installation and setup of control arms, rod-end bushings, and suspension linkages based on weight and performance requirements. Custom machined components — including aluminum uprights, saddle clamps, and hub bearing assemblies — were designed in-house and manufactured to precise tolerances for the suspension geometry.

Manufacturing

Carbon Fiber & Composite Layup

I contributed to the team's carbon fiber and prepreg composite manufacturing process for the vehicle's structural panels and bodywork. This included laying up carbon fiber plies, applying vacuum bagging techniques, and monitoring vacuum integrity with a pressure gauge to ensure proper consolidation. The vehicle floor was constructed as a honeycomb sandwich panel — carbon fiber face sheets bonded to a hexagonal core — providing high stiffness and low weight for the structural floor of the car. The process resulted in lightweight, high-stiffness panels critical to the car's performance targets.

Vehicle Dynamics Team

The Car

The finished vehicle — car #289 — is a full open-wheel electric race car built to SAE Formula specification, featuring custom aerodynamic bodywork, a purpose-built electric powertrain, and the full suite of mechanical systems the team has developed in-house. The car was unveiled at a public reveal event on the USC campus alongside the team's prior combustion car.
